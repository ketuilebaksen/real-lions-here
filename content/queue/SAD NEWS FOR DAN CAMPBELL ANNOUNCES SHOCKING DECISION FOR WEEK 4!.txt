The fit is obvious: he's comfortable in man-to-man, he plays with physicality, and that's exactly the profile this secondary is missing. But there's a real catch. Fulton signed a two-year deal in Kansas City last year and it just hasn't developed: eight games, two starts last season, and now he's buried in a room with first-round pick Mansoor Delane, second-year corner Nohl Williams, and veteran L'Jarius Sneed. He actually helped his stock with a first-quarter interception against the Colts on Sunday Night Football, and the timing works for Detroit: he's on an expiring contract, so this is a rental, not a marriage. If Delane keeps playing well, Kansas City could happily flip Fulton for a pick. Keep monitoring that one.

Number three is Tyson Campbell of the Browns. He was dealing with an ankle heading into Cleveland's week two game against Tampa Bay but played through it, and the résumé is real: 74 appearances, 73 starts across six seasons, seven career interceptions. The difference with Campbell is the contract. He's signed through 2028, so this isn't a rental, it's a commitment. But if Cleveland decides it wants draft capital and starts moving veterans as the deadline approaches, he's exactly the kind of proven starter Detroit should be calling about.

And the fourth name comes with a Detroit twist: Mike Jackson of the Panthers. He actually played one game for the Lions as a rookie back in 2019, after Dallas took him in the fifth round that year, and he's bounced through four teams since. Here's the catch: the Panthers won the NFC South last season and have real expectations again, so they're not moving him just to move him.

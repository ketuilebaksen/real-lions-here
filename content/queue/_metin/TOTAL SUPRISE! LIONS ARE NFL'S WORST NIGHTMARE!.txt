Start with the stage itself, because it's a character in this game. New stadium, first real game in it, red end zones back by popular demand, a fan base that travels through walls. Dan Campbell isn't hiding from any of it. He's embracing it. His message was basically: this is where we want to be, under the lights, best against the best, a night that city deserves, and an environment you tell your grandkids about. But he also drew the line that matters: it's an opportunity to show our conviction, not to put on a show. More concerned with putting on a clinic. He even compared Bills Mafia to our own fans: loud, all in, they show up. He knows exactly what's coming, and he told his guys to enjoy every bit of it, because playing the best against the best in an environment like that is part of the deal, the stuff you never forget. One practical note the national crews keep flagging, though: the grass at the new place was a real issue during the preseason, so keep an eye on the footing tonight.

And Josh Allen? Completely unbothered. Asked about the emotion of opening the new building, the former MVP shrugged: we've got a job to do, it's like any other game, we have to go win. The only thing he'd give up about the palace? The grass is nice. That's a scary level of locked in.

Now let's talk about the Buffalo team, because this isn't last year's Buffalo. Sean McDermott is gone, Joe Brady is the head coach now, and his debut was about as clean as it gets: a 36-31 win in Houston, a building where Josh Allen had never won. He was 0-4 lifetime against that Texans defense, and he responded by picking them apart for over 300 yards with a clutch late touchdown, in a game where Houston scored over 30 and Allen simply had to out-duel them. That's the scary part. This team was already confident. Now their quarterback just exorcised his one regular-season demon in week one of a new era.

And Brady finally gave Allen the thing he hasn't had in three years: a true number one receiver. DJ Moore, traded over from Chicago in March, five catches, 100 yards and a touchdown in his debut. And don't overlook the history: Moore played for Brady in Carolina, he knows this system, and it showed immediately. That connection is week-one-ready in a year where most receiver-quarterback pairs are still calibrating.

Which brings us to the matchup that decides this game, and I'm not going to sugarcoat it, Lions Nation: our secondary against that passing game. Because let's be honest about week one. Through two and a half quarters we looked unbeatable: 21-0, a shutout in progress, 111 yards allowed in the first half. And then the wheels came off the wagon. Tyler Shough, a second-year quarterback on a banged-up Saints offense, threw it 56 times and turned into prime Drew Brees in the fourth quarter. Chris Olave went for 10 catches and 182 yards on us. D.J. Reed and Rock Ya-Sin were in the blender all afternoon. Everyone was catching passes: receivers, tight ends, backs. If you give Tyler Shough that kind of opening and survive, fine. Give it to Josh Allen and you know how that movie ends. And the bigger-picture worry the analysts kept circling is the same one from the offseason: staying healthy on defense, and proving the play calling works in a post-Ben Johnson world. For what it's worth, they think Drew Petzing is the guy, with Campbell keeping a hand in the calls, and nobody doubts what Goff and St. Brown are. But you have to close. You cannot hand a three-touchdown lead back to anybody in this league.

The flip side: the pass rush showed up. Five sacks against the Saints, four of them after halftime, and the national guys are pointing at the same plan we are. The route to slowing Allen is pressure and chaos: zone blitzes, disguised looks, trying to trick him into the turnovers he occasionally gifts you. And Aidan Hutchinson has reportedly had this one circled all offseason. He wants Josh Allen. Detroit has never beaten Buffalo in the Hutchinson era, and the reporting says he knows his sack numbers against them, too. On paper the secondary is undermanned. So the front has to make this ugly. Period.

Now flip it to our offense, because the national reporting out of Detroit is blunt: the long-term RB2 plan can wait, and tonight the Lions are simply going to load Gibbs up. He's the center of this offense, and the hope is that his explosives can go punch for punch with Josh Allen's.

And that's the part every Lions fan is smiling about: Jahmyr Gibbs, the bell cow. Twenty-nine carries, 156 yards, three touchdowns in the opener, plus five catches. The national shows did the math and gasped: that's a pace of something like 538 touches, when Christian McCaffrey led everybody with 413 last year, and no back in NFL history has ever hit 500 carries in a season. But here's the thing: this was the plan all along. It's why Detroit traded David Montgomery to Houston, a deal that, by the way, brought Juice Scruggs to this offensive line. Campbell said back in June that Gibbs would be the bell cow, and week one proved he meant it. The caution is real, though: Gibbs isn't built like Derrick Henry. He's a space player, thick enough to run through arm tackles, but his magic is making people miss at the second level. Feed him, yes. Just remember 500 touches is how seasons end in November.

Especially because the insurance situation is what it is. Isiah Pacheco is on injured reserve until at least December after back surgery. Sione Vaki got the RB2 snaps in week one and struggled in pass protection, and the analysts were brutal about it: that's an immediate exit off the field for a backup back. Campbell says the Vaki-Saylors split is still being talked about. On a night where Buffalo's front will be hunting, whoever stays in to block for Jared Goff matters more than usual.

There's one more soft spot to attack on their side, though. Buffalo has a new defensive coordinator too, Jim Leonhard, and week one showed real communication issues in that secondary. The best way to exploit that? The guy who does it every single week: Amon-Ra St. Brown. Ten catches and two touchdowns in the opener, patrolling the short and intermediate zones, finding every hole in coverage. If Leonhard's back end is a step slow on its checks tonight, the Sun God is going to eat. And with Jameson Williams stretching the field and Gibbs coming out of the backfield, Detroit has the tools to score with anybody.

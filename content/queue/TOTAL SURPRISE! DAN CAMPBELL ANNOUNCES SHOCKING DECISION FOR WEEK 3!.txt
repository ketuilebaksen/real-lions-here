Let's set the scene first, because the opener had everything. Detroit went up 21-0, Jahmyr Gibbs ran wild, and it looked like a stroll. Then New Orleans caught fire, tied it 24-24 with a minute left, and we all aged five years. The Saints even got a shot at a 62-yarder as time expired and missed it. In overtime they scored, and Kellen Moore decided he wasn't playing for a tie: two-point conversion, ballgame on one snap. Tyler Shough's throw hit the ground, and the Lions walked out 1-0.

And Campbell's breakdown of that final play is exactly why I love listening to this man. It wasn't one hero. It was three levels working together: Tyleik Williams getting push right up the middle, Derrick Barnes taking away the underneath hook throw, and now the quarterback has to hitch and go over the top, where Rock Ya-Sin is hanging in the window. Campbell said the situational work paid off all night, from being smart with six seconds left in regulation, no freebies, make the kicker hit a real long one, to the defensive staff burning timeouts before that two-pointer, narrowing down what was coming. And on Rock, who admitted he'd been through a drive from hell earlier? Campbell's words: he doesn't get rattled, he doesn't shy away from competition, he doesn't lose confidence. He just keeps coming back for more. That's your game-sealing stop.

Now the schedule gods hand us Buffalo, on the road, Thursday night, their home opener after a big win in Houston. Campbell didn't sugarcoat it. He knows Joe Brady very well, he called the quarterback the catalyst of that whole operation, and he went out of his way to praise the offensive line, the weapons, the back. And then the part that should get your attention: he called Buffalo's defensive line THEIR catalyst. Ed Oliver. Greg Rousseau, the length, the explosiveness, coming off a monster opener. Bradley Chubb on the other end. A big boy in the middle. An active front, aggressive special teams, an electric building on opening night. Campbell's exact vibe: we've got our hands full, and this is a big-time game early in the year.

Which brings us to the uncomfortable part, because that monster front is arriving at the worst possible moment for this offensive line. Christian Mahogany picked up an injury in the opener. Campbell doesn't think it's serious long term, but he admitted flat out it could be tough to get him ready for Thursday. His answer on the depth was calm, though: we've got Bartch in there, we've got Juice, we've got Frazier, we can keep going for him. And then the line that tells you everything about how this front office thinks: that's why we made the moves we made in the offseason.

Blake Miller is the other one, and yes, the rookie right tackle is a concern. Campbell was honest about the short-week math: the day after a game, especially the first game of the year, you barely know where guys are at. Is there a chance Miller plays Thursday? His answer: yeah, for sure, and he'd actually feel better about Miller than Mahogany if he had to pick one. But he also gave us the cruel truth of Thursday football: some guys would be ready if the game were Friday. Just not Thursday.

So let's talk about Ben Bartch, because he's suddenly very important. He stepped in for Mahogany in the opener and played more than anyone expected. Campbell's review was fair: some real good, some not so good, inconsistent, a little rusty, which is normal for a guy who barely got reps until last week. He's a veteran, and Campbell expects a lot more of the good moving forward. Four days from now, against Rousseau and Chubb, we find out.

Now the part that made every Lions fan smile: Sam LaPorta. He jumped off the screen on the very first drive, and Campbell called him a huge part of what this offense did, right alongside the tight end room as a whole. Detroit rushed for around 160 yards, and the tight ends had a hand in that. But listen to how Campbell talked about the man himself. That is a hard road back, from a herniated disc and back surgery to fully healthy, through nearly a full training camp, to game one. Credit to the staff, sure, but more importantly, credit to him. The way he works, the way he takes things seriously, everything he does to be the best player he can be. Number 87 is back, and it showed.

There was one wart Campbell owned, and it matters for Thursday: the Saints got Detroit with tempo in that fourth quarter. Guys got gassed, packages got caught on the field. His response? We're anticipating it, because Buffalo does that stuff anyway, and why wouldn't they test us there? We would. The fix on a short week is all mental: walkthroughs, planting that seed of urgency, showing them the looks they're going to get, and then trusting instincts on Thursday night.

He was honest about the sloppy stuff too. Going in, his big message was pre-snap discipline, and one offsides aside, which they overcame on a long scoring drive, they were pretty good. But there was a holding call that hurt, and Campbell went full boxing coach on it: first game of the year, butterflies, guys start throwing haymakers instead of the jab they've thrown all camp. The tough part is there's no time to clean it up properly. They're already into Buffalo prep, the grade sheets are handed out, and the real corrections wait for the weekend, when you're cleaning up two games at once. That's short-week life, and Buffalo is living the same one.

Speaking of workload: Gibbs carried it 29 times for 156 yards and three touchdowns, and when Campbell got asked how the short week changes his usage, he opened with make sure you drink plenty of Gatorade. The serious answer: everybody's in the same boat after game one, four days of recovery lines up with when they'd be going full speed in a normal week anyway, and Buffalo just came out of the same Sunday. He thinks Gibbs will be recovered, ready, plenty of juice in the tank.

Behind him, though, the RB2 story took a turn. Sione Vaki played the most offensive snaps of his career, and it was a mixed bag: a couple of good carries, a decent checkdown, and some pass-protection reps that were not good, including the one everybody's talking about. Campbell didn't hide from it: can't do that, he knows it, we've got to clean it up, because we're going to need him. Him AND Saylors.

And what about Jacob Saylors, the man who won the preseason and then barely saw the field? Campbell says it wasn't a statement, it was a choice: give Vaki the shot, because he's the heavier back, he can carry, he can protect, and he's quick with good hands in the pass game. Instead of sprinkling both guys in, they doused with one. But he left the door wide open for this week: maybe Saylors gets those reps, maybe they split it, they're literally still talking about it. Not a done deal. Real Lions Here translation: that RB2 role is still live, and Thursday might look completely different.

One more piece of news buried at the end of that presser: Isiah Pacheco. Asked for a timetable, Campbell said it's going to be a while, a significant amount of time, and the reporting around it says why: back surgery, with a recovery window that could stretch toward December. So that RB2 conversation isn't a two-week problem. It's the season.

So here's where we stand, Lions Nation. 1-0, ugly and beautiful at the same time. A defense that made the one stop that mattered, a superstar back who carried the day, LaPorta back to being LaPorta, and real questions on the right side of the line walking into an electric Thursday night against a front built to punish exactly that. I'm watching Miller and Mahogany's status, I'm watching how this team answers tempo, I'm watching the Vaki-Saylors split, and I'm watching Bartch against that Buffalo front most of all.

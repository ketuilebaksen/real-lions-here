Story one: the depth moves. Detroit signed offensive guard Elijah Klein to the practice squad and brought wide receiver Dominic Lovett back to the organization.

Klein is 26 and spent his first two NFL seasons in Tampa Bay, who took him in the sixth round of the 2024 draft. He appeared in 26 games, started one, and played 123 snaps on special teams and 93 on offense. His only NFL start came last season, week three against the Jets, when he played 72 snaps. Modest numbers, sure. But what makes Klein interesting is the versatility. He's played left guard, right guard, and primarily center in the NFL, saw preseason action at center in 2025, and this preseason he lined up at both guard spots AND right tackle.

For a practice squad player, that flexibility is gold. If injury hits this line, Klein is one guy who can plug multiple holes. Detroit already has Larry Borom at tackle, Seth McLaughlin at center, Ben Bartch at guard, and Miles Frazier bringing his own versatility, with Mason Miller and Devin Cochran opening the season on the practice squad too. Klein is another option that costs the Lions nothing on the 53. That's how you build a line that survives December.

Then there's Dominic Lovett, back after a short absence, and this one's a little emotional if you followed cutdown weekend. He didn't make the initial 53, and he was left off the first practice squad announced on Monday. That had to sting. But a few days later the phone rang again, and now he's back in the building with something to prove. Detroit drafted him in the seventh round out of Georgia last year, and he came into this season as a bubble player after an inconsistent camp.

The preseason told the story. Against Cincinnati: three catches on eight targets for 28 yards, with two drops. Against Washington: 14 snaps, one catch on two targets for six yards, but no drops. Against Indianapolis: 15 snaps, zero targets, and a 49.6 offensive grade from Pro Football Focus. That's a trend line going the wrong way, and Dan Campbell had already spelled out what he wants from Lovett: consistency. Not just the occasional play, but all the small details this staff demands, every single day.

So why does this return matter? Because the path back is real. Detroit is carrying just four receivers on the active roster: Amon-Ra St. Brown, Jameson Williams, Isaac TeSlaa and Tay Martin. And the Lions released Greg Dortch, who's since landed with the Buffalo Bills, which leaves a genuine need at punt returner. Lovett is young, athletic, and competitive. Show Campbell the consistency, handle the details, and there's a game-day elevation sitting right there on special teams. This is his chance to prove he belongs.

Now story two, and this one has the immediate impact: the Lions placed running back Isiah Pacheco on injured reserve and signed veteran tight end Tyler Conklin back to the active roster.

Pacheco missed most of camp with a sprained MCL, and there was some early hope he could still make week one. Then Dan Campbell revealed the part that changes the tone: Pacheco has also been dealing with a back injury, and the back was the bigger concern, more than the knee. On injured reserve, he now misses at least the first four games of the season. No opener against the Saints, no early rhythm, just rehab and patience.

That puts the spotlight squarely on the room behind Jahmyr Gibbs. Gibbs has his new contract, the expectations are sky-high, and he's set up for another monster season. Pacheco was never going to inherit the exact David Montgomery role anyway, so Gibbs was always positioned for a serious workload. But you still don't want to pile everything on your star back in September.

Which is exactly why Jacob Saylors matters right now. He had an impressive camp and preseason, and he looks like the clear leading candidate for the backup job. He went over 100 yards on the ground in the finale against the Colts and finished the preseason averaging 5.7 yards per carry on 23 attempts. He can return kickoffs too, which suddenly matters more for this team. Campbell's take was simple: Saylors benefited from finally getting real opportunities to carry the football, and he took full advantage of them. Now the opportunity in front of him is much bigger.

Behind Saylors, Detroit still has Sione Vaki and Jabari Small around. Vaki didn't play a single preseason snap, but Campbell said the work he got late in camp was very good, and Small is a familiar name the coach kept bringing up when he talked about this room. For now, though, the path is clear: Saylors has the inside track to RB2 while Pacheco is out.

And then there's the Conklin move, which I love. A week ago, after the cuts, I told you I'd be watching Jackson Meeks in a suddenly thin tight end room. Detroit clearly saw the same thing, because after releasing Conklin when the initial roster was finalized on Sunday night, one Pacheco IR move later, they brought him right back. The tight end room is now Sam LaPorta, Brock Wright, Tyler Conklin and Jackson Meeks. Meeks is the fascinating one, the undrafted receiver who moved to tight end this offseason, added more than 20 pounds to survive the position, and keeps making contested catches. But Conklin brings the one thing that room was missing: experience.

He's 31, a Michigan native, a former Central Michigan standout, with eight years in the league. A spring calf injury delayed his start to camp, but before that he was working high in the rotation during OTAs and looked like a natural fit in Drew Petzing's offense. Since 2021 he's caught 10 of his 11 career touchdown receptions, and from 2021 through 2023 he put up at least 58 catches and 552 yards every single season with the Vikings and Jets. That's not just another body. That's proven production.

And it matters because of what Petzing likes to do. In Arizona, he leaned on jumbo formations and squeezed serious production out of Cardinals tight end Trey McBride. Give him LaPorta, Wright, Conklin and Meeks, and Real Lions Here can already see it: four different body types, four different jobs, matchup problems on every snap.

So here's the bigger picture. Detroit lost a real piece of its running back depth the moment Pacheco hit injured reserve, and now Saylors and that room have to keep this offense balanced behind Gibbs. Conklin walks back into a tight end group that could quietly become a weapon in this scheme. Klein gives the line another versatile safety net. Lovett gets more time to grow and a special teams door standing wide open. None of these moves dominates a headline on its own, but this is what building a roster for a long season looks like.

And the standard is different now. This team isn't trying to figure out what it has. The Lions believe this is a legitimate Super Bowl window. St. Brown, Jameson Williams, Gibbs and LaPorta are the headliners, Petzing adds a new layer to the system, and the talent is obvious. But the deeper you go into a season, the more the bottom of the roster decides your ceiling. Pacheco's injury is the perfect example: one setback, and suddenly the next man has to be ready. That's Saylors' reality today. Lovett's is proving the consistency Campbell keeps asking for. Klein's is staying ready. Conklin's is being the steady veteran in an intriguing room.

I'm not panicking over any of this, but I am watching all of it closely, because when you're chasing a Super Bowl, the small decisions in September have a way of becoming enormous in January. The talent is here. Now the depth has to hold up its end. We waited a long time for Detroit to carry this kind of pressure. Now it's time to handle it.
